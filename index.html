I build systems that read: models that look at an image, recognize the symbols in it, and turn them into correct language.

I graduated in Computer Science and Engineering from Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET), Bangladesh. My undergraduate thesis was a system that reads Arabic Braille from photographs and converts it back into Arabic text, published at IEEE ICCIT 2025.

The thesis taught me where these systems actually break. The recognizer reached 96% accuracy per character, yet whole sentences still came out wrong. A single misread cell could derail an entire word, and rebuilding valid Arabic from a correct stream of characters, with its diacritics, ligatures and word boundaries, was a hard problem on its own. Getting perception right is not the same as getting language right, and that gap is what I want to work on.

I am applying to CS PhD programs for Fall 2027. If you are recruiting in vision, NLP, or vision–language research, I would be glad to hear from you.

Research interests

  • From perception to language How small recognition errors in vision–language pipelines grow into wrong text, and how models can detect and recover from them instead of passing them along.
  • Reliable generation in low-resource languages Evaluating and improving language models for languages like Bangla and Arabic, where benchmarks are thin and unfaithful output is hard to catch.
  • Efficient adaptation for specialized domains Fine-tuning small models with parameter-efficient methods for fields such as clinical text, where data and compute are limited.

Publications

A Comprehensive System for Arabic Braille Interpretation

Md Ashfaqur Rahman, Muhammad Sheikh Sadi

International Conference on Computer and Information Technology (ICCIT), IEEE, 2025, pp. 2992–2997

A two-stage optical Braille recognition system for Arabic. We extended the existing dataset to cover under-represented characters, diacritics and ligatures, tuned a custom CNN classifier, and added a post-processing stage that rebuilds Arabic words from the recognized cells. The classifier reaches 96% per-character accuracy.

Selected Projects

Doctor–patient dialogue to SOAP notes

Fine-tuned Flan-T5-base with LoRA to turn medical conversations into structured clinical notes (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan). Only 0.71% of the model's parameters were trained, and the full run took about 45 minutes on a single T4 GPU, using 10,000 dialogue–note pairs.

Mancala with an AI opponent

A playable Mancala (Kalah) game in Python and Pygame. The computer player chooses moves with alpha-beta search over the game tree, including extra turns. A fuzzy-logic model estimates the human player's chance of winning as the game goes on, and a genetic algorithm suggests the human's best next move.

3D football stadium in OpenGL

An interactive 3D stadium with a simple shooting game, written in C++ with OpenGL 3.3 and custom GLSL shaders. It implements Phong lighting with directional, point and spot lights, textured models, a hand-built sphere mesh for the ball, a free-moving camera, and collision detection with the goal.
